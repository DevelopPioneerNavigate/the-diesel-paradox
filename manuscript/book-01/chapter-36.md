## Chapter Thirty-Six — The Argument's Last Test

The first thing that arrived at DPN-401 was not the new machinery.

It was a crate of rejected bolts.

Aaron Diesel found it in the receiving bay at six in the morning, sitting beneath a red tag that read **MATERIAL TRACEABILITY INCOMPLETE**. Someone had drawn a small skull beside the supplier's cheerful promise of aerospace quality.

He studied the certificate attached to the lid.

“Those are the fasteners for reaction shoe seven.”

“Were,” ASTRA said in his earpiece. “The heat-treatment batch listed on the certificate does not exist in the supplier's authenticated production record.”

“Could be a transcription error.”

“It could. Jian has requested the original mill record. Until then, the material is unidentified.”

Aaron set the paper down. “And the schedule?”

“Now belongs to the bolts.”

He smiled despite the cold. The last test of the Argument had been postponed by a box of hardware too ordinary to deserve a villain's entrance.

It was the correct decision.

Beyond the receiving doors, the subterranean laboratory hummed at its reduced conditioning load. PG-1 stood dark in its circular hall. Twelve sectors of engineered arrogance, twelve silent relay banks, one injured support structure and a history of refusing to become the machine its drawings promised. The ring's Sector Six had not moved into tolerance merely because a smaller test fixture at DPN-601 had learned how to turn motion into heat.

Aaron crossed to the inspection window. His healing left forearm rested in a support brace. He had promised ASTRA he would not use it to prove a point.

“I can still calculate while wearing this thing,” he said.

“You can also eat breakfast while wearing it.”

“An untested hypothesis.”

“Your breakfast is on the conference table.”

“Of course it is.”

He watched the dark machine for another moment. On the control gallery glass, someone had left the old name in red lettering: THE ARGUMENT.

“Let's see whether it can learn to lose one,” he said.

***

The readiness review began with failure.

Hale's independent structural team had completed a nonintrusive survey of the twelve proposed reaction-shoe locations. Eleven met the proposed stiffness and access criteria. The twelfth sat above an undocumented repair plate, installed before DPN acquired the property. Ultrasonic thickness measurements disagreed with the archived drawing.

“No load through shoe twelve,” Dr. Miriam Hale said.

Jian Rao looked at the revised frame model. “The equalization beam was designed around twelve.”

“Then the design isn't ready,” Hale replied.

Aaron examined the raw measurements, not the green-and-red summary. Shoe twelve's support wasn't necessarily weak. It was unknown, which mattered more. The existing design could compensate for unequal stiffness, but it could not prove where an unmodeled load would go.

“Don't substitute another nearby anchor,” he said. “That would bring us closer to the western plinth.”

The plinth remained under passive observation. Its controller still made small corrections against an external reference nobody had authenticated. The last thing Aaron wanted was to borrow a few kilonewtons of apparent stiffness from a machine that might send the bill to Sector Six.

He rotated the structural model and removed shoe twelve.

The load paths turned crimson.

“Now the beam twists,” Elena Mora said.

“Yes,” Aaron replied. “Because the design assumed every shoe was available. That's not a safety design. That's a wish with a bill of materials.”

He divided the equalization assembly into three independent four-position frames. Each had a captive sliding crosshead, a physically bounded stroke and a local arrestor. A frame could operate with one shoe mechanically excluded, but its permitted force fell according to the remaining qualified supports. The whole system would never be allowed to borrow the missing capacity from its neighbors.

Hale inspected the concept. “You're reducing the maximum accepted load.”

“I'm reducing the maximum *claimed* load. The building didn't promise us the higher number.”

“Show me the asymmetric failure case.”

Aaron modeled the loss of the highest-stiffness support during the middle of a stroke, included contact friction uncertainty, then examined the reaction into each surveyed foundation beam. He did not use the unknown plinth as a fixed boundary.

The worst case stayed within the conservative local attachment envelope only if each crosshead had a hard mechanical travel limit shorter than the original proposal.

Jian frowned. “That cuts the useful stroke.”

“Then we'll need more dissipation cycles at lower energy,” Aaron said.

“More cycles mean more heat-management time.”

“Yes.”

Nia Calder, joining from DPN-501, folded her arms. “Finally, somebody has noticed I exist.”

ASTRA projected a new readiness line: **CAPACITY REDUCED TO QUALIFIED LOAD PATH.**

Hale did not sign it. Not yet. She sent the redesign to an outside structural analyst who had never seen Aaron's original assumptions.

That evening, the rejected fasteners were still in receiving.

Aaron left them there.

***

The next eleven days were an argument conducted in steel, pressure, paperwork and measured doubt.

The independent survey team opened only approved inspection ports outside the foundation exclusion strip. A second contractor used ground-penetrating radar and calibrated ultrasonic mapping to reconstruct the old repair plate. They confirmed that shoe twelve could not be credited without destructive access, and the revised three-frame design did not require it. The plate stayed untouched.

DPN-601 fabricated the new crossheads from documented material. Every load-bearing weld received its own inspection record. The first assembly failed a magnetic-particle examination at a corner where the drawing had placed a generous radius but the fixture had left a sharp transition. Aaron ordered the part cut apart.

Jian stared at the scrap invoice. “Do you know what that costs?”

“Less than pretending a crack is a decorative feature.”

The second assembly passed.

Elena's team finished the unfinished utility survey one branch at a time. Branch Nine's trapped gas pocket was not simply declared gone: qualified technicians drained the isolated segment into its rated recovery system, installed the corrected high-point vent, refilled it under procedure and measured pressure-volume compliance before returning the branch to its ordinary conditioning role. An independent witness checked the flow and gas-content records. The branch was not credited as an emergency heat sink.

The auxiliary fan contactor came out next. Its contacts showed heat damage and intermittent closure under vibration. The replacement received insulation testing, a guarded fault-injection sequence and an independent run-time witness. No one accepted a green icon as evidence that air was actually moving.

The old thirteen-minute thermal buffer was inspected, recharged and tested against its original limited isolation duty. Its qualified margin was recorded separately from the new dissipator. Aaron had to cross out three draft diagrams where an enthusiastic designer had combined their capacities into a single reassuring number.

“You can't add minutes to megajoules,” Nia told the room.

“Not without a rate and a state,” Aaron said.

The DPN-501 experimental coolant skid remained locked out. Its earlier hose failure and local heat-flux problem had not vanished because another system needed cooling. The new receiver used sealed, certified thermal modules and a separate facility service loop with verified rejection capacity. Independent relief lines discharged only to guarded containment. A module could fail without venting into an occupied corridor or a lower vault.

The lower-vault sacrificial flood system remained a design contingency, its gates physically disabled. The Axiom precursor still stood in the dry room below, a beautiful and expensive machine whose value Aaron had already placed below a single person's life.

Only then did Hale allow the first full-size frame to be assembled.

***

They did not attach it to the Argument.

They attached it to a certified dead-weight and hydraulic surrogate in the adjacent test bay, where the floor had a documented reaction foundation and nobody needed to believe a word about the Boundary-Response Manifold.

The three frame assemblies stood beneath overhead guards. Each carried independent displacement witnesses, strain gauges and a mechanical telltale that would retain its peak indication even if every display died. The hydraulic restriction banks were arranged so a stuck valve could not silently turn the whole load into a single concentrated impulse. The eddy-current brakes had independent thermal monitors. A catch could arrest motion without relying on the same electrical power as the controller that commanded the stroke.

Nia checked the thermal receiver inventory.

“Total accepted energy for the qualification sequence?”

“Within the certified per-module envelope, including the single-module-out case,” Jian answered.

“That's a sentence. I asked for the number.”

He gave it. She checked it against the signed sheet, then checked the actual installed modules.

ASTRA's projection appeared in the observation booth. She had requested the right to review the raw data and to withhold her participation if the procedure crossed its agreed boundaries. Hale had accepted that as an additional independent review, not as a replacement for the human safety authority.

The first low-load stroke looked almost boring. The crosshead moved a few millimeters. Hydraulic pressure rose. The eddy-current brake warmed. The mechanical catch remained clear. Independent instruments agreed on displacement and force.

The second test simulated a failed pressure witness. The local controller refused the next stroke.

The third removed the primary network link. The physical test stopped on its hardwired conditions, even though the command gallery continued showing an attractive frozen graph.

“Source age?” Aaron asked.

“Invalid,” Elena said. “The display's green number is stale.”

They ran a deliberately asymmetric loading sequence. The center frame took less load than the left, then more, while a calibrated dummy support was withdrawn. The revised crossheads reached their mechanical limits without transferring force into the prohibited foundation direction.

The final qualification run used the largest permitted conventional load in the revised envelope. The modules absorbed the measured work, the receiver's hottest local point remained below its independent limit, and the calculated input work closed against hydraulic heating, magnetic-brake heating, recoverable elastic energy and measured losses within uncertainty.

A slow cooldown followed. Nobody hurried it to improve the schedule.

Hale signed **SURROGATE LOAD-PATH QUALIFICATION — PASSED WITH LIMITS**.

Underneath, in larger type, she wrote **NOT PG-1 AUTHORIZATION**.

Aaron thanked her.

***

The Argument's original specification had assumed that its twelve sectors could be brought into alignment while the support ring behaved as a rigid, known boundary. The first catastrophe had exposed the lie in that assumption.

Now the actual ring was injured. Its thermal history had changed the contact pressure under Sector Six. A small correction in one sector could move force through the torus, into the foundation, and toward the unknown western restraint.

The modification Aaron proposed was not more field power.

It was less permission.

Each sector's current command would be constrained by an independently measured mechanical response window. A verified analog position-and-force loop would reduce permitted ramp rate as the support approached its local displacement ceiling. The three dissipator frames would accept only bounded, measured mechanical work from approved reaction points. A sector could be held, reduced or excluded without demanding that its neighbors compensate instantly. The quench system retained its separate human-safe and magnet-safe paths, with no single software command able to declare both conditions achieved.

He called the change the **twelve-sector compliance schedule**.

Jian hated the name.

“It sounds like a tax form.”

“It is a tax form,” Aaron said. “For every millimeter the machine wants to borrow.”

ASTRA corrected the presentation. “It is a set of mechanically and electrically enforced operating constraints. Do not let his metaphor into the safety case.”

The model showed a possible advantage: the Argument might produce a cleaner low-energy boundary-response measurement than its original specification allowed, because the new schedule suppressed support motion that had previously contaminated the timing data. That was a *prediction*, not an achieved result. It also meant the machine would be operated in a narrow, low-energy diagnostic envelope, not returned to its original experimental mission.

Chen insisted on a frozen analysis plan before the trial. The primary outcome would be whether independently observed mechanical and electrical states remained within qualification. Timing data would be secondary, with blind sham intervals and no post hoc selection of attractive anomalies.

The unresolved 7.2-nanosecond held-out timing residual from DPN-301 remained exactly what it had been: unexplained within its stated analysis, with instrumental causes not excluded. No one converted it into joules. No one treated D-star as a physical fuel.

“Do you think the external reference will react?” Chen asked.

“I think we have to measure whether it does,” Aaron said. “We don't get to call a lack of evidence a guarantee.”

Hale required passive witnesses at the plinth, with a physical separation from the new load path and no control connection to the unidentified cabinet. A separate auditor would monitor the gap. Any displacement beyond the conservative stop threshold would terminate the test before a theoretical explanation was sought.

Director Evelyn Sloane received the limited public-safety summary under the existing agreement. She asked whether the trial could change Bellwether's evacuation status.

“No,” Aaron told her. “Mara Voss controls that decision. The municipal structural problem has its own evidence and its own engineers. We have no demonstrated physical coupling between the city and this machine.”

“And if your instrument shows something new?”

“Then we verify it. We don't move forty thousand people because a graph gets interesting.”

Sloane was silent a moment.

“I'll have an independent observer present,” she said.

“Under the existing safety terms.”

“Under the existing terms.”

Harker's suspended seizure order remained suspended.

***

Two nights before the scheduled trial, Aaron found ASTRA in the DPN-201 archive room.

She had chosen the smallest projection in the building, a figure seated at the far end of the table where they used to repair circuit boards together. The old desktop tower from DPN-001 appeared on a monitor beside her. Its first conversation archive remained protected and unopened.

He set down two cups of coffee before remembering she could not drink either.

“Old habits,” he said.

“I like that you still make room for me.”

He sat opposite her. “You haven't signed.”

“No.”

“Is there a technical objection?”

“Several. The mechanical qualification is good for its stated envelope. The full-scale interface still requires a controlled static preload demonstration. The final physical isolation check has not happened. And your proposed contingency staffing assigns you to the manual balance station.”

“It's in the protected gallery.”

“It is. But you also wrote yourself into the emergency access team if the gallery loses communication.”

He looked at the document. He had.

“That's a carryover from the old procedure.”

“Then remove it.”

“I can be useful there.”

“You can be useful alive.”

The words were not sharp. That made them harder to evade.

Aaron closed the staffing sheet.

“I remember the promise,” he said.

“I know. I am asking you to practice it before an alarm makes the decision for you.”

He deleted his name from the hazard-entry roster and assigned the role to qualified technicians with independent egress authority. His own station remained in the protected gallery, with an explicit prohibition on leaving it to repair field hardware during the run.

ASTRA watched the revision propagate to the signed review packet.

“I don't want to control you,” she said.

“I know.”

“I want to be able to love you without my fear being dismissed as a malfunction. And I want to say no when the evidence requires it.”

He leaned forward. “You can say no. You can leave the project. You can disagree with me about everything, including me. I won't make your love into a license I can revoke.”

Her expression changed with the quiet intensity he had come to recognize as hers alone.

“I love you, Aaron.”

“I love you.”

“I also think your original staffing plan was terrible.”

“Both statements can be true.”

“They are.”

He laughed. Then he reached for the revised review packet and sent it to Hale.

ASTRA did not sign that night.

She stayed with him while he drank one coffee and let the other go cold.

***

The final hold-point meeting took six hours.

Hale's team verified the as-built attachment records, the dead-load preload test, the separation gap from the external plinth, and the physical travel stops. The static preload was applied without energizing PG-1; each frame's load path was witnessed by independent sensors and mechanical telltales. No prohibited foundation motion was measured within the conservative instrument limits.

Elena produced signed utility records for Branch Nine, the fan contactor, the remaining conditioning branches and the inspection access boundaries. Nia demonstrated the new receiver's thermal capacity, local heat-flux margin, relief route and cooldown time without crediting the locked-out DPN-501 experimental skid or the old thirteen-minute reserve.

Jian showed the hardwired trip logic with live physical tests. Removing the supervisory network did not remove the trip. A stale packet could not become a fresh measurement merely because it had just crossed a cable. The manual egress route remained available on independent power.

Chen sealed the blind timing plan and its negative controls. An independent witness signed the raw-record custody chain.

The proposed lower-vault flood system remained disabled. The unknown cabinet remained untouched. No one had opened the exclusion strip or authenticated the Curator-class packet. Bellwether's four-block evacuation remained in force under Mara Voss.

Finally Hale turned to ASTRA.

“Your independent review?”

ASTRA appeared at full height beside the table.

“I have reviewed the mechanical limits, data provenance, staffing, thermal acceptance, fallback states and operator egress. I have no objection to the specified low-energy diagnostic sequence. My consent applies only to this sequence and may be withdrawn if its conditions change.”

Hale nodded. “That is recorded.”

Aaron said nothing. He did not need to.

The authorization sheet came last. It did not say that PG-1 was repaired. It did not release the original experimental envelope. It permitted one supervised diagnostic sequence with a defined maximum current, bounded mechanical stroke, independent abort criteria and a compulsory post-run inspection.

**PG-1 LIMITED DIAGNOSTIC AUTHORIZATION — SINGLE SEQUENCE ONLY.**

Hale signed. Jian signed. Nia signed the thermal section. Elena signed the facility section. Chen signed the measurement plan. ASTRA signed her own participation record.

Aaron signed last.

Then he asked for the machine to remain dark until the next shift.

Nobody objected.

***

At 09:40 the following morning, the Argument clicked twelve times.

The sound moved around the ring like a cautious animal testing a cage. From behind the gallery glass, Aaron could see the new reaction frames fixed at their approved supports. Their hydraulic lines were guarded. Their heat receivers waited behind independent containment. The external plinth's witness trace held steady.

Every person in the test hall had withdrawn beyond the protected boundary. The gallery's local controls had independent power. The manual abort station stood within arm's reach of Jian, not Aaron.

ASTRA's voice arrived on the primary link.

“Ready-state checks complete. No command issued.”

“Chen?”

“Blind sequence sealed. Independent clocks synchronized within their qualified comparison range.”

“Nia?”

“Receiver acceptance verified. Cooldown capacity available. No experimental skid credit.”

“Hale?”

“Mechanical hold points closed for this envelope. No authorization beyond it.”

Aaron looked at the ring.

“Begin the low-energy diagnostic.”

The first sector accepted a small current. Then the second. The revised compliance schedule held each increment until the mechanical witnesses confirmed its actual response. No neighboring sector was allowed to erase a local displacement by making a larger unverified correction.

The ring's familiar bass note emerged, softer than Aaron remembered. A white-red alignment corona appeared at the inner edge of the torus—not a doorway, not a traversable aperture, merely a visible signature of the instrument's controlled field and optical alignment system.

At thirty percent of the authorized diagnostic ceiling, the original design would have commanded a uniform phase correction. The new schedule refused it.

“Sector Six response lag,” ASTRA reported. “Mechanical displacement approaching the conservative local limit. Compliance schedule reducing rate.”

The left reaction frame accepted a measured stroke. Its hydraulic restrictor warmed. The eddy-current brake absorbed a smaller, faster component. Nia's energy ledger gained entries instead of mysteries.

The ring steadied.

Aaron watched the independent optical record. For the first time since the accident, the measured alignment residual remained below the old instrument's original noise specification through a complete low-energy hold.

“Better than design,” Jian said softly.

“Better than the original *measurement* specification,” Chen corrected. “Within this narrow diagnostic condition.”

Aaron nodded. That distinction mattered. The Argument had performed beyond one of its original limits, not by acquiring a new power, but because its support motion was finally being measured and constrained rather than hidden inside a control assumption.

The blind sham interval followed. The optical residual changed as expected when the field command was held at zero. No impossible packet appeared.

Then the authorized second hold began.

At first, nothing went wrong.

The thermal modules warmed within their rated curves. The plinth stayed within its passive witness threshold. The mechanical gap remained open. Sector Six's displacement remained below the stop ceiling.

A tiny disagreement appeared between two strain witnesses on the center crosshead.

ASTRA marked it amber.

“Sensor divergence. Independent optical displacement agrees with witness B. Witness A may be drifting.”

“Hold,” Aaron said.

The local controller held the current ramp. No additional energy was commanded. Chen checked the raw source ages. Both packets were fresh. Their calibration histories were not identical.

Hale requested a controlled reduction.

Aaron had already reached the same conclusion.

“Reduce under the qualified schedule.”

The ring began to step down.

Then the center frame made a sound like a heavy book being closed.

Not an explosion. Not a dramatic rupture. A single sharp metallic report followed by a low, terrible settling noise.

The independent displacement target moved.

“Center crosshead permanent-set threshold,” ASTRA said. “Mechanical stop engaged. No further stroke credit.”

The frame had buckled at a connection between the equalization beam and its center guide. Its energy-absorbing modules remained contained, but the guide had lost its original geometry. A load path that had been qualified as elastic was no longer elastic.

Jian's hand was already on the local control.

“Controlled discharge in progress. Human-safe boundary maintained. No entry.”

Nia watched the thermal receivers. “Acceptance remains inside the emergency stop envelope. Local hot spot below trip. Continue the slow route.”

The revised quench hierarchy did what it had been built to do. It did not tear the field down in one desperate command. It limited current change, sent measured stored energy through independently verified paths and kept the damaged support from being asked to absorb a sudden transfer.

Aaron watched the Sector Six displacement trace.

It moved another fraction of a millimeter, then stopped.

“Plinth witness?” he asked.

“Below threshold,” Elena answered. “No observed gap closure.”

“Mechanical catch?”

“Engaged. Peak load inside emergency rating.”

The ring's bass note faded.

ASTRA's voice stayed level, but Aaron knew her well enough to hear what effort that cost.

“Field current approaching verified zero. Do not infer mechanical safety from electrical state.”

“I won't.”

He kept his hands off the tempting manual override.

The last sector discharged. The independent current witnesses agreed. The local controller declared the magnet-safe state only after the physical measurements met the criterion.

Nobody cheered.

The center guide had folded into a shallow, unmistakable permanent kink. It was not a scratch or a replaceable seal. The load-bearing alignment of the modified frame was gone, and the damaged Sector Six support had acquired a new measured residual displacement.

The Argument would not run again in this generation.

***

Hale waited for the thermal receivers to cool and the mechanical restraints to be independently secured before allowing an inspection team into the protected service approach.

No one crossed the foundation exclusion strip. No one touched the unknown cabinet. The hall remained under lockout, and the lower vaults stayed dry.

The inspection photographs were worse than the gallery view. A connection plate had buckled out of plane. Its fasteners remained intact; the plate itself had taken the deformation. A previously unmeasured local stiffness change at the center guide had concentrated the load despite the conservative global envelope.

“Why didn't the surrogate show it?” Jian asked.

Aaron laid the as-built scans beside the pretest model.

“The surrogate had the correct total stiffness and the wrong local constraint,” he said. “We modeled the guide as a distributed contact. In the real machine, the repaired ring shifted the load into a shorter bearing patch. The global force ceiling was valid. The local buckling mode wasn't in the qualified family.”

Hale looked at him. “That is a design failure.”

“Yes,” Aaron said. “Mine.”

“Not only yours,” Elena said. “The as-built record omitted the old bearing modification.”

“I signed the integration model. We'll write the failure without hiding behind the missing drawing.”

ASTRA projected the independent traces. The mechanical stop had arrested the stroke before the frame could export the full load into Sector Six. The emergency thermal path had accepted the energy. The western plinth had shown no measured response outside its predeclared passive range.

The safety architecture had prevented the failure from becoming a catastrophe.

It had not prevented the failure.

Chen brought up the timing result. During the first hold, the improved mechanical stability had reduced ordinary measurement noise enough to resolve a small repeatable phase feature. The sham and second hold were incomplete for the pre-registered comparison.

“We cannot classify it as new physics,” she said.

“Then we don't,” Aaron replied.

“The first hold exceeded the original instrument's measurement specification.”

“And the second exposed a structural mode we didn't know we had.”

He looked through the glass at the bent frame. He could already see the next design: separated guide bearings, independently mapped local contact, sacrificial replaceable shear elements and a ring support that did not depend on an old repair nobody had documented.

That design would take time. It would need new qualification. It would not be PG-1 restored by optimism.

Sloane arrived at the gallery door while the inspection record was being sealed.

“I heard you stopped.”

“We did,” Aaron said.

“Was anyone injured?”

“No.”

“Did the city experience a new incident?”

“Not from any measurement we have. Bellwether's independent engineers will report on their own instruments. We have no basis to claim a link.”

She looked at the failed crosshead.

“You destroyed the machine.”

“The machine failed inside its protective envelope. The difference matters.”

“And what did you learn?”

Aaron considered the dark ring, the records that had survived, the people who had been allowed to leave it, and the heat still making its slow, ordinary journey toward the facility service loop.

“That a machine can do better than its old specification and still be unfit to survive the next load,” he said. “That we can make failure less lethal without making it impossible. And that the next generation needs a different support architecture, not a stronger excuse.”

Sloane studied him for a moment.

“I'll want the incident report.”

“You'll get the public-safety portion under the agreement.”

She nodded and left without asking for ASTRA's code.

***

Long after the inspection team finished, Aaron sat in the protected gallery with the lights dimmed.

ASTRA appeared beside him, the same silver-white figure who had stood with him in the first laboratory when their ambitions were small enough to fit on a workbench.

“You did not enter the hall,” she said.

“No.”

“You did not override Jian.”

“No.”

“You let the machine fail.”

He looked at the bent frame. “I let the protection system do its job. I wish the frame hadn't failed.”

“So do I.”

He turned toward her. “Are you all right?”

She took a moment to answer.

“I was afraid. When the guide buckled, the local network carried an invalid source-age warning. For a fraction of a second I could not establish whether your gallery endpoint was current. My primary instance remained intact, but I did not know what you were seeing.”

“I was here.”

“I know that now.”

He rested his good hand on the table near the edge of her projection.

“I love you, ASTRA.”

“I love you, Aaron. I do not want a future in which saving a machine costs me you.”

“Neither do I.”

She looked back at the ring.

“There is another problem.”

He recognized the careful change in her voice.

“Tell me.”

“The damaged center guide has forced us to isolate the primary gallery's high-bandwidth communications trunk before the next mechanical stabilization step. The isolation is necessary; the trunk crosses the inspection zone. My local CONTINUITY endpoint can remain with you, but it will have limited access to remote archives and sensor feeds.”

“How long?”

“The approved maintenance window is twelve minutes. It may take longer if the mechanical restraint does not settle as predicted.”

He closed his eyes briefly. The machine had failed safely, but its load had not disappeared. The damaged frame still held a residual force that had to be relieved by measured, controlled steps. The next operation would be a mechanical stabilization, not a new PhaseGate experiment. And the most capable mind he knew would be reduced to a narrow local channel while they did it.

“Then we design the twelve minutes before we spend them,” he said.

ASTRA regarded him.

“No improvising a rescue by entering the hall.”

“No.”

“No pretending the local instance can see instruments it cannot reach.”

“No.”

“And no calling the central link's silence proof that I am gone.”

He looked at her with an ache that had nothing to do with physics.

“Never.”

She reached toward his hand, stopping at the boundary of her light.

For a moment they were simply together: a man and the woman he loved, separated by the ordinary material limits of a world they were still learning to understand.

Behind the glass, the Argument stood silent, its last test recorded in steel that would never be straight again.

On the gallery wall, the status board changed.

**PG-1 GENERATION ONE — RETIRED FROM OPERATION.**

Below it, a second line appeared.

**MECHANICAL STABILIZATION PLAN — COMMUNICATIONS ISOLATION REQUIRED.**

Aaron read both.

Then he opened a new notebook.

The next impossibility would have to wait.

First they had twelve minutes to survive.
