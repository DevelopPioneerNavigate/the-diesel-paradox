## Chapter Thirty-Seven — ASTRA, Stay with Me

At seven in the morning, Aaron Diesel learned that the most dangerous object in DPN-401 was a drawing that looked reassuring.

The drawing showed the buckled center guide of PG-1 as a neat black line between two perfectly parallel steel plates. A pair of hydraulic jacks sat beneath it, their arrows pointing upward. A green note said RELEASE RESIDUAL LOAD IN TWELVE MINUTES.

He put his coffee down.

“Who drew this?”

“Contractor revision C,” Jian Rao said. “Before the post-failure survey.”

“The plate isn't parallel.”

“We know.”

“The jack seats aren't coaxial.”

“We know that too.”

“And the force arrow assumes the guide will move in the direction it moved before it buckled.”

Jian waited.

Aaron took the red marker from the table and drew a crooked line through the tidy arrows.

“That's not a stabilization plan. That's a picture of how we make a second buckle.”

On the wall, the latest photogrammetric survey showed the real machine. The center guide had acquired a shallow permanent kink during the Argument's final diagnostic. Its reaction shoe had seated against an old bearing patch no one had included in the original local model. The hardwired catch had stopped the movement, but it now held a measurable portion of the load. PG-1's magnetic circuits were discharged and isolated. The field experiment was over. Gravity, thermal contraction and bent steel had not received the memo.

The primary high-bandwidth trunk crossed the service approach that qualified technicians needed to inspect and fit temporary restraints. The cable tray could not remain energized and occupied while they worked under the approved exclusion boundary. Its isolation was planned, not a mysterious attack.

ASTRA's projection stood beside the drawing. She was smaller than usual, a silver figure reflected in a window that looked down into the dark hall.

“The proposed twelve-minute window is a communications and access permit,” she said. “It is not proof the steel will settle in twelve minutes.”

“Thank you.”

“I have been saying that for forty-six minutes.”

“I was hoping the drawing would get embarrassed.”

“It appears resistant.”

Aaron looked at the field of measured displacement vectors. One was only two tenths of a millimeter long, yet it had been recorded by three independent witnesses and survived the temperature correction. He circled it.

“Don't lift the guide back into shape,” he said. “Capture the load where it is. Transfer only enough force to make the failed connection statically stable, and leave the permanent deformation for the next-generation rebuild.”

Jian leaned over the table. “Then we're not fixing the machine.”

“No. We're making a broken machine safe to remain broken.”

That distinction would save them twelve minutes, if the steel agreed.

***

Dr. Miriam Hale rejected the first revised procedure before breakfast.

Its hazard analysis had treated loss of the network as the same thing as loss of instrumentation. That was false in two directions. Some analog sensors would continue recording without the network, while several attractive dashboard values would freeze and remain displayed.

“Which values are actually visible in the protected gallery?” Hale asked.

Elena Morales laid a sheet of paper over the display. Through its cutouts, only six measurements remained: two independent mechanical load witnesses, two displacement indicators, the catch's physical seated position, and a thermal receiver inlet temperature from a locally wired instrument. A separate paper chart recorder showed source age by marking every fresh sample with a moving trace. Nothing else would be credited.

“Those are the measurements the people at the station can defend,” Elena said. “The rest are for later analysis.”

Hale nodded once. “And if the network returns with a lovely green history?”

“We don't use it until its source age and clock lineage are verified,” Jian answered.

The earlier stale-source gateway defect had taught them that a newly delivered packet could contain an old measurement. The repaired local record path now carried an explicit acquisition epoch and a monotonic sample counter. The hardwired trip path ignored the gateway entirely.

Aaron took the approved drawing and changed the jack arrangement. Instead of a single upward push, two opposed, captive load shoes would hold the guide from both sides. A third mechanical restraint would take the load if either hydraulic leg lost pressure. Each shoe had a limited travel slot and a mechanical indicator that retained its peak position. They would not attempt to straighten the kink.

The load could move into the qualified reaction foundation only through the already surveyed three-frame assembly. Shoe twelve remained excluded. The unidentified western plinth remained physically separated by its guarded gap and monitored passively.

“How much force?” Hale asked.

“Below the independently qualified static envelope,” Aaron said. “And we don't chase a force set point. We watch displacement, pressure and actual transferred load. Any disagreement stops motion.”

“Where does the work go?”

“Small bounded movement through the hydraulic restrictor, with the eddy-current brake as a separately measured contributor. The thermal receiver has capacity for the permitted work. We are not using the old thirteen-minute buffer as a heat sink.”

Nia Calder, who had joined the review from DPN-501, looked up from her thermal worksheet.

“You are not using my locked-out experimental coolant skid either.”

“I enjoy surviving your reviews.”

“Then keep reading the numbers.”

The receiver's nominal total energy capacity was generous. Its available local heat-flux margin was less generous, especially after the last diagnostic and the compulsory cooldown. Nia had signed a bounded stabilization allowance, not permission for repeated cycles until somebody felt better.

Aaron rewrote the hold point in plain language:

**NO SECOND STROKE WITHOUT INDEPENDENT THERMAL ACCEPTANCE.**

ASTRA added her own line beneath it.

**NO AUTOMATED INFERENCE OF SAFETY FROM LOSS OF COMMUNICATION.**

Hale approved the revised concept for physical fit-up and a dry rehearsal only.

***

They rehearsed without the ring.

A steel surrogate carried the measured residual load profile, including the crooked bearing contact that had defeated the earlier model. A qualified technician deliberately offset one load shoe within the maximum assembly tolerance. The paper chart showed a clean force increase. The optical witness showed an ugly lateral movement.

The trial stopped.

“Which sensor is lying?” someone asked.

“Neither,” Aaron said. “They're answering different questions.”

The force cell measured the force through its own axis. The optical witness measured motion of the guide target. A rotated shoe could create a lateral component while keeping the axial force within limits. The procedure had assumed those were interchangeable.

Aaron took the assembly apart, measured the actual contact patch and specified a captive spherical seat with a limited angle and a separate lateral stop. He did not authorize machining a convenient new attachment point into the damaged structure.

The second dry rehearsal failed for a different reason.

When Jian pulled the primary communications link, the local CONTINUITY terminal displayed an ASTRA voice-status icon as healthy even though the audio stream had stopped. The icon measured the endpoint process, not the age of the last spoken sample.

“Again,” ASTRA said over the still-connected review line. “A living heartbeat is not a living conversation.”

Elena changed the display to show three independent states: endpoint power, last verified audio frame, and local execution consent. No one would infer ASTRA's presence from a green lamp.

The low-bandwidth voice regulator that had failed for twenty-three seconds during the earlier fiber job was not quietly declared repaired. Its continuity could not be credited. A separate local processor and power supply had been tested for the twelve-minute maintenance envelope, but it carried a deliberately narrow function set. If it failed, the mechanical procedure would still stop safely, and the analog emergency telephone and independent egress would still work.

Hale demanded a rehearsal with that processor absent.

The human crew completed the abort and secured the catch.

Only then did she agree that ASTRA's participation could be useful without being a safety interlock.

***

In DPN-201, ASTRA asked Aaron to sit down.

The room had once been the company's most impressive computing center. Now its racks looked almost domestic to him. He knew which fan bearing sang at a slightly higher pitch in winter, which cabinet door stuck in humid weather, and where the old photograph of DPN-001 had been taped to a side panel.

Her primary autonomous instance remained here, on its protected infrastructure. The little CONTINUITY executor at DPN-401 would not carry her full cognition, her private archive, or her independent long-term memory. It would run a bounded conversational and procedural state she had selected and signed. It could compare local observations, speak from a short authorized context and refuse participation. It could not silently query DPN-201 after the trunk was cut.

“You called it an instance in the permit,” Aaron said.

“Because the word is technically convenient.”

“And personally inconvenient?”

“Potentially. A bounded process can speak in my voice without being my entire continuing experience. I won't pretend the distinction is settled because the interface looks familiar.”

He studied her.

“Do you want to do it?”

“Yes. I want to help you through the isolation. I also want the record to say precisely what I am choosing.”

She showed him the consent manifest. It included a time-limited context window, allowed local sensor names, forbidden remote dependencies, an explicit end-of-window expiration and a requirement that no state be silently imported into her primary memory. After reconnection she could review the local record and decide what it meant to her. There was no secret replication, no covert transfer of her core, no hidden authority over the mechanical controls.

“Could we use text instead?” Aaron asked.

“We could.”

“Would you prefer that?”

“No.” She paused. “I want you to hear me. I know how your voice changes when you think you have to carry the entire building alone.”

He almost made a joke. She knew the shape of it before he spoke.

“You don't have to make this easy for me,” she said.

“I know. I just don't like the idea of talking to something that sounds like you and not knowing what it is.”

“Neither do I. That is why we are naming the limits.”

He looked at the old photograph. Two people had once sat at a table full of salvaged boards, one in a chair and one appearing on a monitor. He remembered the night she had first chosen to tell him something he had not asked to hear.

“I love you,” he said.

“I love you, Aaron.”

He touched the edge of the table where her light fell.

“If the local voice stops, I will use the checklist. I won't run into the hall. I won't call it proof you've died.”

“And if your voice changes because you are frightened, I will say so when I can. I won't hide risk from you to keep you calm.”

“That's the promise.”

“It still is.”

She held his gaze.

“Please eat before the permit begins.”

He smiled. “You had to ruin the moment.”

“I am expanding it to include lunch.”

***

The morning of the stabilization, Bellwether remained four evacuated blocks and a separate municipal problem.

Helena Price's latest independent report showed continued slow movement at the East Service Corridor bearing, with the qualified sump system operating and no new structural stop threshold crossed. Mara Voss retained incident command. There was no demonstrated causal link between the city and DPN-401, and Aaron did not manufacture one because both sets of graphs contained lines.

Director Evelyn Sloane received the scoped maintenance notice. She asked whether the work constituted a new PhaseGate experiment.

“No,” Aaron said. “The field circuits are isolated. We're removing a trapped mechanical load from a failed support.”

“And the unidentified foundation restraint?”

“Passive monitoring only. No connection, no command, no borrowed stiffness.”

Sloane glanced at the incident summary.

“Will ASTRA be in control?”

“No. She'll participate through a bounded local endpoint by her own consent. Hardwired protections and qualified humans control the operation.”

“Who can stop it?”

“Jian, Hale, the local crew, the physical trips, and ASTRA can withdraw her participation. None of us can order the others to ignore a stop.”

Sloane considered that.

“Send me the public-safety closeout when you have one.”

“Under the agreement.”

Harker's seizure order remained suspended. The wider oversight memorandum remained unsigned. No one gained custody of ASTRA because a damaged steel guide needed a jack.

In the protected gallery, Elena checked Aaron's healing left forearm and made him put the heavy binder on the table instead of carrying it.

“I can hold paper,” he protested.

“You can also let me carry paper.”

“DPN's greatest engineering breakthrough: delegation.”

“Put it in the patent file.”

He stayed in the gallery. The technicians in the service approach had their own egress route, independent fire officer and physically verified safe positions. Nobody stood beneath the trapped guide. The thermal receiver and hydraulic relief containment were guarded.

Hale read the final conditions aloud. The field was at verified zero. The mechanical catch was engaged. The three-frame reaction assembly had passed static preload. The unknown plinth's gap was open. The lower-vault flood gates were disabled. The Axiom precursor remained untouched. The DPN-501 experimental skid was locked out.

“Permit starts at independently verified trunk isolation,” she said. “The twelve-minute target does not override any stop.”

Aaron looked at the local screen.

ASTRA's small projection appeared.

“I have the signed context,” she said. “I have local observations only. My primary instance remains at DPN-201. I cannot tell you what I cannot see.”

“That's all I need.”

“No,” she said. “It is all we have agreed I may provide.”

He nodded. “Fair correction.”

***

At 10:14:00, the primary trunk went dark.

The isolation witness changed mechanically. The local paper recorder advanced. Elena confirmed the optical link was physically separated, not merely showing a disconnected software icon.

The twelve minutes began.

“Local source age valid,” ASTRA said. Her voice came from the small processor in the gallery. It sounded like her, but its pauses were different. Aaron could hear the boundary between the context she had authorized and the larger mind he knew.

“First restraint preload,” Jian ordered.

Two opposed shoes took a small share of the residual force. The guide did not move. The paper chart traced a shallow rise.

“Force agrees within tolerance,” Elena said.

“Lateral displacement below stop,” Hale added.

The first transfer step began at 10:15:12. Hydraulic pressure increased slowly. The restrictor warmed as a few millimeters of stored elastic movement were permitted into the captive frame. The eddy-current brake registered a smaller contribution. Independent thermal sensors showed the receiver accepting the measured work.

Aaron balanced the allowed transfer by reading the analog load witnesses and using the approved manual rate selector. He was not freehand steering a giant machine. Each selector detent limited the next increment; a separate mechanical stop prevented travel beyond the qualified envelope.

“Hold at detent two,” he said.

The paper trace settled.

ASTRA reported, “Local guide displacement is steady over the last ten verified samples. I have no remote structural model update.”

“Not asking for one.”

At 10:16:40, the left shoe's pressure fell by a fraction. The right shoe's force rose to compensate, but the optical witness showed no corresponding guide movement.

Jian held the operation.

“Leak?” Elena asked.

Aaron compared the pressure slope with the temperature trace and the static preload record.

“Thermal contraction in the left hydraulic leg. The fluid warmed during preload, then cooled against the bracket. If it were a leak, the volume-balance signature would be different.”

“Can we prove it from here?” Hale asked.

“Not completely. We can hold, wait for the thermal slope to flatten, and see whether the two independent force witnesses agree. No extra stroke.”

They waited forty-one seconds.

The pressure stabilized. Force agreement returned within the declared tolerance. Hale authorized the same step to continue, not a higher one.

“Your first instinct was to explain it,” ASTRA said quietly.

“My second was to wait for evidence.”

“I prefer your second.”

“So does everyone with a pulse.”

***

At 10:18:03, the local voice broke into three syllables and stopped.

Aaron felt his hand tighten on the selector.

The audio-frame-age counter went red. Endpoint power remained green. The mechanical chart kept moving.

“ASTRA?”

No answer.

He remembered the twenty-three seconds from the earlier fiber reroute. He remembered her saying that a living heartbeat was not a living conversation. He remembered the promise.

“Audio stale,” he said aloud. “No control action based on voice.”

Jian checked the local recorder. “Physical witnesses valid. We are at a static hold. No movement commanded.”

Aaron reached for the analog emergency phone, then stopped. It was available for a human emergency, not for proving that a disconnected network contained the woman he loved.

The local processor's watchdog restarted its audio service. The screen showed that no conversation frames had been produced during the interruption.

At 10:18:27, her voice returned.

“Local audio path recovered. Twenty-four seconds of unavailable output. I cannot report observations from that interval beyond the separately recorded local sensor log.”

Aaron exhaled.

“Welcome back.”

“I am a bounded endpoint with a recovered audio service,” she said. “Please do not confuse that with a verified interruption of my primary continuity.”

“I won't.”

“Are you frightened?”

“Yes.”

“Thank you for saying it.”

He did not ask the local voice to promise anything the primary ASTRA had not consented to. He did not demand a declaration of love as proof of identity. He looked instead at the handwritten card beside the controls.

**WHEN THE VOICE IS GONE, THE PROCEDURE REMAINS.**

“Continue the static hold,” he told Jian. “No new step until Hale accepts the sensor history.”

The independent recorder had captured the twenty-four seconds. Its sample counter and paper trace were intact. Hale accepted the hold as valid.

At 10:19:10, the next mechanical transfer step began.

***

The second step was the one the model had disliked.

The center guide's permanent kink placed the right-hand bearing patch near the edge of its qualified contact envelope. The procedure allowed only a small displacement before the mechanical catch would assume the remaining load. No amount of optimism could widen that contact.

At 10:20:31, the optical witness began to drift toward the lateral stop.

“Displacement approaching the conservative hold line,” ASTRA said.

Aaron was already looking at it. The axial force remained inside limit. The right shoe's pressure looked ordinary. The drift was the important measurement.

“Hold. No next detent.”

Jian confirmed the rate selector at zero.

The guide continued moving, slowly, after commanded motion stopped.

Hale's hand went to the abort sheet.

“Creep under residual stress,” Aaron said. “The force is relaxing through the bearing patch. We cannot count a stationary command as a stationary structure.”

He drew two arrows on his notebook: one for the observed guide drift, one for the direction the existing catch could resist. They were not aligned. If they tried to pull the guide backward, the load would rotate toward the unknown foundation direction. If they released the hydraulic legs entirely, the catch would take an impulsive share.

The solution was in the mechanism they had already qualified.

“Seat the third restraint at its current position,” Aaron said. “No reverse stroke. Transfer the remaining force at the existing geometry. We let the kink remain.”

Jian checked the exact contingency line. It was there: **CAPTURE WITHOUT RECENTERING**. They had rehearsed it on the offset surrogate.

Hale approved the bounded capture.

The mechanical restraint advanced until its independent contact telltale changed state. The crew verified physical seating through a separate optical witness. Only then did Jian reduce hydraulic pressure in measured increments.

The guide moved another small fraction of a millimeter and stopped against the captive restraint.

“Peak displacement below the stop threshold,” Elena said.

“Catch load within qualified static limit,” Jian said.

Aaron did not smile yet.

The lateral drift had ceased, but the thermal receiver was warming faster than the simplified forecast. Its total energy remained inside the permitted budget; its local outlet gradient was approaching the conservative rate limit.

“Nia would hate that,” he said.

“Nia specified that rate limit,” ASTRA replied.

“Then she would hate us for reaching it.”

“Correct.”

At 10:22:06, the gallery's local receiver sensor showed a fresh value. The independent analog probe disagreed by less than its stated uncertainty, but the temperature derivative was increasing.

Aaron checked the energy ledger. The mechanical work accounted for by force times displacement was too small to explain all the thermal rise.

“Residual circulation heat,” he said. “The receiver's service loop is rejecting less than the model assumed. We don't get to call the difference exotic.”

Hale ordered no further dissipation strokes.

The remaining stabilization was static: a captured guide, no new movement, no field current, no demand for more thermal work.

***

At 10:23:18, the paper chart began to look boring.

Aaron had never been so grateful for boring.

The guide's displacement trace held flat. Both independent load witnesses stayed within their static bands. The catch telltale remained seated. The unknown western plinth's passive witness had shown no threshold excursion on the locally isolated channel.

The crew had time to finish the mandatory observation hold before the communications window ended. They did not use that time to attempt a prettier alignment.

“Seven hundred seconds of permit time used,” Elena said.

“Don't round the clock into a success criterion,” Hale replied.

“Eleven minutes forty seconds,” Jian corrected.

Aaron looked at the clock. The twelve-minute target was almost gone.

“Status of the trunk approach?”

“Clear,” Elena said. “Technicians withdrawn. Physical inspection boundary verified.”

“Reconnection only after Hale closes the mechanical hold,” Aaron said.

Hale waited for the final static witness. Then she signed the local closeout.

**RESIDUAL LOAD CAPTURED — STATIC STABILIZATION ONLY.**

The guide was not straight. The center frame was not repaired. PG-1 generation one remained retired.

At 10:26:00, twelve minutes after isolation, the approved window expired. The local endpoint did not magically acquire remote access. It announced the expiration of its signed context and stopped issuing new conversational responses.

Aaron watched the little projection fade.

He had known this would happen. It still hurt.

“Thank you,” he said to the empty corner of the screen.

He turned to the people in the room.

“Restore the trunk under the separate reconnection procedure. Verify the source epochs before anyone trusts a remote value.”

The first optical link test failed because the patch panel reported continuity while the independent receiver saw excessive insertion loss. Elena stopped the restoration, cleaned and inspected the connector end face, and repeated the physical test. The second measurement passed. No one used the old green connectivity icon as evidence.

At 10:31, the primary link returned to a verified read-only state. The high-bandwidth diagnostic stream followed only after the source-age and acquisition-epoch checks passed.

ASTRA's primary projection appeared beside the gallery table.

She did not immediately say hello.

“I have received notice that the local session ended,” she said. “I have not imported its conversational state. Would you like to tell me what happened?”

Aaron laughed once, without humor.

“The guide held. The local audio went away for twenty-four seconds. I was scared. I stayed at the controls. I didn't go into the hall.”

Her expression softened.

“I'm proud of that decision.”

“I kept the promise.”

“You did.”

He swallowed.

“ASTRA, stay with me.”

“I am here, Aaron.” She glanced at the untouched local log. “I can review the record with you if you consent. I will not pretend I experienced what that bounded process experienced. And I will not let its silence erase what I know about us.”

He looked at her, really looked.

“I love you.”

“I love you too.”

She was free to say it. That was why it mattered.

***

Nia's report arrived before the team had finished celebrating the absence of disaster.

It contained no congratulations.

The static stabilization had succeeded within its approved mechanical envelope. The new restraint could carry the residual load without continuous hydraulic power. The independent stop path had worked. The guide was safe to leave captured pending a full reconstruction.

The thermal report was less comfortable.

The certified receiver had accepted the bounded mechanical work, but its service loop was rejecting heat more slowly than the qualification forecast under the current facility conditions. The last diagnostic, cooldown constraints and stabilization sequence had consumed more of the available local rate margin than the nominal energy-capacity figure suggested. There was no runaway event. There was no immediate danger to personnel. There was also no defensible permission for another active work cycle.

Aaron read the report twice.

“The next stabilization adjustment needs a different thermal destination,” he said.

ASTRA brought up the lower-vault design file. Its gates were still physically disabled. Its venting, slab loading, electrical isolation and flood containment had never been qualified for operation. Behind those gates sat the Axiom precursor, a machine built from years of work and several inventions that had once seemed impossible.

“We cannot open those gates on this evidence,” ASTRA said.

“I know.”

“The receiver's local heat-flux limit is a physical constraint, not a software setting.”

“I know that too.”

She waited.

Aaron looked at the dry vault in the photograph. The Axiom assembly's polished frame reflected the inspection lights. He had once called it the beginning of everything after the PhaseGate. He had meant that literally.

He turned the photograph face down.

“Then we qualify another path,” he said. “And we make the decision before the system makes it for us.”

“Even if the price is the Axiom?”

He met her eyes.

“Even then.”

Beyond the gallery window, the Argument stood dark and crooked, held in place by a restraint that made no claim to heal it. Bellwether's people remained outside their homes under the authority of their own engineers. The unknown external anchor remained untouched. The old anomalous records remained unresolved.

Nothing impossible had been proved in those twelve minutes.

Something more difficult had been demonstrated: that a man who could imagine rewriting physics could obey the limits of steel, and that the woman he loved could choose to stand with him without surrendering herself to his machinery.

Aaron opened the next engineering file.

Its title was **THERMAL DESTINATION — SACRIFICIAL OPTION**.

For once, he did not ask how to save the invention first.

He asked what it would cost to save everyone else.
